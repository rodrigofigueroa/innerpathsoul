Emotional Health

Emotional health could be the key to a better world

✦ Mónica Schekaibán AssadPsychotherapy · Emotional Health

The expression of our emotions — such as fear, happiness, anger, sadness and many others — was taboo for years and was reserved for moments of intimacy or for psychotherapy. Today, however, we can express them in daily life, and thanks to emotional intelligence we have managed to do so more assertively.

Whether we are parents, teachers, workers, or simply interested in improving the quality of our relationships, we are fortunate to be at the beginning of a new era in communication and emotional health.

Learning to manage our own frustrations and disagreements with others, developing our ability to listen and being open to learning from our relationships, are essential factors for our emotional health.

Emotional management and human well-being

This change in our communicative habits could be the key to a world with less violence and fewer conflicts — a world in which the inner peace of each human being could lead to peace among people.

Listening with empathy means being totally available, recognizing and reflecting the emotions and needs of one another. Being heard with empathy allows feelings of clarity, relief and trust in others and oneself to emerge; it leads to adaptation and healing.

Specialists around the world have demonstrated that the way human beings manage our emotions has an impact on our physical and mental health.

02

Human Development

The importance of the birth experience

✦ Mónica Schekaibán AssadHuman Development · Maternity

Birth involves the baby's separation from the mother's body, and at that exact moment the independent life of the new human being begins — our first perception of the world that receives us.

«Rather than humanizing birth, we must mammalify it.»

Michel Odent

The impact of the way we are born will reverberate throughout our entire life. The experience the mother has during pregnancy is fundamental: from the treatment she received from her loved ones, to the level of stress, to whether the baby was wanted or not.

The arrival of a baby should in all cases be a wonderful experience, of love, of sharing life. For this reason it is important to educate our daughters to live this process with happiness — that birth is something normal, beautiful, transformative and even pleasurable.

Giving life is a divine gift — a spiritual moment full of meaning and magic. It is a moment to honor, give thanks and transcend — recognizing that moment as unique and unrepeatable, where the world begins to change and evolve.

Psychology · Human Development

The socialization process in personality development

✦ Mónica Schekaibán AssadPsychology · Life Stages

«Each human being carries within themselves an ideal person, just as each piece of marble contains a statue in rough...»

José Martí

The development and formation of personality occurs throughout the entire life of the human being from birth to death. The sociocultural and historical circumstances lived at certain stages of life determine the characteristics of each person.

Infancy — 0 to 12 months

The moment a baby is born, it begins to have involuntary, spontaneous and impulsive movements. Unconditional reflexes ensure the child's survival in the face of new environmental demands. By the end of the first year, the child has achieved sensory development, the foundations for language, and standing unsupported.

Early Childhood — 1 to 3 years

The child feels great curiosity about the surrounding world and explores it enthusiastically. They seek increasing independence and begin differentiating object functions. Personality development shows recognition of oneself as an independent being.

Adolescence — 11 to 20 years

The adolescent goes through a stage of deep experiences and significant internal and external changes — one of the most critical moments of personality development. There is emotional instability expressed in constant difficulties in relationships with adults, irritability and hypersensitivity.

In later adulthood, the family and community are maximally responsible for keeping the older adult socially active. The person needs self-transcendence: they seek to live on in others, to feel that their life has not been in vain.

Philosophy · Spirituality

What is the meaning of BEING?

✦ Mónica Schekaibán AssadPhilosophy · Consciousness

Being is what «is». To be is everything that possesses a «soul». It is the essence of something, everything that has life; the difference lies in the value given to each life.

«To be or not to be, that is the question.»

William Shakespeare, Hamlet

BEING is:

  • The essence — all beings are manifestations of the spirit, of the one.
  • Metaphysical individuality — the energy carried by its atoms according to the degree of light housed in its consciousness.
  • A unique and unrepeatable entity. It is self-aware, free, and can self-determine.
  • It has dignity as an absolute value. It is a social being by nature, transcendent and unrepeatable.
  • Its attributes: love, intelligence and power.
  • It has awareness of its greatness and its limitations. It has desires to live more and better.
The soul is not analyzed. The soul is listened to. It is the deep intelligence that inhabits every person — the part that knows, that feels, and that remembers. Being is not built from the outside; it is discovered from within.

Personal Development · Vocation

Can I turn my work into a vocation?

✦ Mónica Schekaibán AssadPersonal Development · Purpose

Work is everything a person does for their satisfaction, joy and well-being; all those activities that satisfy primary needs, ranging from material to spiritual wealth.

What is vocation?

Vocation is the taste or inclination one has toward a profession or career. It is a state of happiness that comes from doing what one likes and gives full satisfaction and joy. It is the inspiration the individual hears from an early age or at any moment of their life path.

The positive implications of work

  • Social and personal identity
  • Status and prestige
  • Meaningful social relationships
  • Personal development — putting knowledge and skills into practice
  • Providing power and control over one's own life

During the age of cathedral-building, a man came upon three stonemasons. The first said he was cutting stone. The second said he was building a capital. The third said he was building a cathedral. All three were doing exactly the same thing — but the meaning was entirely different.

When one lives work as a vocation, one enjoys it more, finds meaning in it, and feels how it positively contributes joy to one's life and those around them. Work, if you turn it into a vocation, becomes a place of learning and personal growth.
