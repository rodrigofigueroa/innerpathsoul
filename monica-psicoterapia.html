Individual Therapy

Personalized accompaniment

A safe space to explore your emotional world, your thoughts and beliefs. We work with what you live, what you feel, and what you want to change.

The initial sessions (4 to 6) aim to identify the main conflicts and their possible causes, as well as the objectives and work plan.

Emotion Interpretation

Brief therapy · 3 hours

Brief psychotherapy through the interpretation of the person's words. Equivalent to many hours of traditional sessions. A mapping is made and at the end, irrational beliefs and conflicts are identified.

Therapeutic approaches

Types of therapy practiced

🧠

Cognitive-Behavioral

Evaluates behavior by identifying thought patterns that generate discomfort to reprogram them towards more functional behaviors.

⚡

Brief Therapy

Seeks to find solutions to specific problems in a short time. Focused, effective and with concrete results in few sessions.

🌿

Family Constellations

Works through the soul's entire family lineage, acknowledging and honoring inherited dynamics that affect present life.

🔤

Word Interpretation

Recording and mapping of words that oscillate between the conscious and subconscious to find the origin of conflicts.

🧘

Meditation & Imagery

Breathing techniques to lower anxiety and guided meditation. Imagery to decree and live in a positive way.

💡

Rational-Emotive (RET)

Identifies irrational beliefs that generate suffering and develops skills through internal confrontation and reason.

Frequently asked questions

What I'm asked most

Emotional pains that have not subsided — anguish, fear, anxiety, stress, anger, resentment, loneliness, sadness, frustration, low self-esteem, depression — are clear signs that you need professional accompaniment.

To learn to make better decisions, to be more with oneself, to accept reality as it is, to identify conflicts, to detect blind spots, to take responsibility for one's actions, and to build healthy and realistic life projects.

The idea is for the person to learn skills in a few sessions to face conflicts and manage negative emotions. Many tools can be used simultaneously to achieve mental, physical and emotional peace.

Above all, she accepts that she has conflicts so that the treatment works. The person must participate actively, question herself, be patient and brave, and have an open mind to understand her own internal processes.

One has the freedom to talk about what one needs: the suffering experienced, the grief process, conflicts with others, work or family problems, fears, insecurities or anything that generates discomfort.

Therapy is structured so that this does not happen. The therapist does not tell you what to do or make decisions for you. The goal is for the person to find their own answers and develop tools to manage their life independently.

Generally not, because the goal is for the person to find their own answers. However, if specific guidance is needed, it may be offered — but always framed as a tool, never as an instruction.

A good therapist: listens without judgment, uses session time to understand you, charges a reasonable fee, and respects the therapist-patient relationship with confidentiality, respect, ethics and values.
